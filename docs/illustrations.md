# Illustrations

Every place in Haunt that wants a picture, and one prompt that makes them all.

## The style

A **rubber-stamp field note**: warm off-white paper, one small hand-carved stamp
pressed onto it in two or three desaturated inks that sit a hair out of register,
with dry gaps where the pad ran short. The carving keeps only what you would
recognise a place by — a ridge, a tower, a stand of trees — and nothing else.

It suits Haunt because the app is nearly black. A warm paper card on that ground
reads as a physical object someone kept, which is exactly what a haunt is.

Borrowed from [@Hamburgerai's travel field-note
posters](https://x.com/Hamburgerai/status/2090683415104557406) — the idea, not
the images. We make our own.

## No text, and no background

Two rules before anything else.



Image models misspell, and they bake words into pixels at one size, in one
language, for one place. Every asset below is **paper and ink only**. The app
already has its own copy for each of these screens and typesets it in
`--font-mono` over or under the art, which stays crisp at any density and can
carry a real haunt's name instead of a made-up one.

**And no paper.** The first set came back as ink on warm paper, which looked
beautiful and wrong: the app's ground is `#000000`, so every illustration became
a bright rectangle sitting on top of the design rather than part of it. What the
app wants is the impression alone — pale ink, no page under it, transparent
everywhere the pad did not touch.

Both exceptions are the share card at the end, which is a picture *of* the
product and never appears inside it.

## The one paste

Paste this whole thing into a chat-based image model — Gemini / Nano Banana,
ChatGPT, whatever draws best for you — then say **"start with 01"**, and **"next"**
after each one. Staying in one thread is what keeps twenty images looking like
one set.

```
I need a set of 21 images in one consistent style. Here are the rules that apply
to every single one, then the list. Generate them ONE AT A TIME, in order, and
wait for me to say "next" between them. Match the paper and the inks of the first
image exactly in all the others.

=== RULES FOR EVERY IMAGE ===

A single square image of ONE hand-carved rubber stamp impression, pressed onto a
flat pure black ground.

The background is pure black, #000000, edge to edge. Perfectly flat and even: no
paper, no fibre, no grain, no texture, no vignette, no gradient, no lighting, no
shadow, no border, no frame. All of the texture in the image belongs to the ink.
(If you can output a PNG with a transparent background instead, do that.)

The impression sits low and slightly left of centre. It occupies about a third of
the image height and no more than half the width, with generous empty black
around it and clearly more empty space below it than above. The emptiness is the
composition — resist filling it.

The ink is PALE, printed light onto dark, and comes only from this palette:

  #C8C8CC   pale grey        — the main ink, for structure and outline
  #B7D2C6   pale sage green  — foliage, anything living
  #C8C4EE   pale lavender    — water, distance, atmosphere
  #DFBEA4   pale warm tan    — brick, roof tiles, lamplight, warmth

Use two of these per image, three at the very most, and let one of them be a
small accent rather than an equal partner. Nothing outside this palette. No white,
no black ink, no saturated colour, nothing bright.

Each colour is pressed separately, so the layers land about a millimetre out of
register in different directions. Every impression shows its making: carving
marks, uneven gouged line weight, notched and broken outlines, dry patches where
the pad ran short, black showing through the middle of a stroke, grainy pigment,
uneven pressure, and a faint ghost of a doubled press at one edge. Nothing is
smooth, nothing is anti-aliased into a clean vector edge.

Each carving is reduced to the least information that still reads. Delete crowds,
vehicles, rows of windows, repeated buildings, fussy foliage, ornament and
background. It must look carved and pressed — not illustrated, not a logo, not a
woodcut art print, not a line drawing, not a photo with a filter.

NO text, letters, numbers, captions, signatures, borders or frames in any image.

Avoid: paper, parchment, canvas, any visible surface texture in the background,
circular seals, red ink seals, postage stamps, perforated edges, wax seals,
stickers, collage, souvenir templates, smooth vector logos, generic city icons,
dense over-carving, cartoon or children's craft style, 3D rendering, plastic
sheen, digital gradients, glow, drop shadows, saturated colour, mockup
photography, desks, hands, watermarks.
```

### If it hands you a contact sheet

Asked for twenty-one images, a model will often answer with one image containing
all twenty-one, numbered in the corners. The style will be right and the files
will be useless: each cell lands at a couple of hundred pixels, and the numbers
are baked in.

Keep the sheet — it is the style reference, and the hard part is done. Then paste
it back and pull the cells out one at a time:

```
Here is the sheet you made. I need each cell as its own image now.

Take cell 04 and reproduce it as a standalone square image at full resolution.
Keep the paper, the ink colours, the carving and the wear exactly as they are in
that cell — this is a reproduction, not a reinterpretation. The stamp sits low and
slightly left of centre, with generous empty paper around it and more below it
than above. Remove the number. No labels, no grid lines, no borders, no text of
any kind. The paper fills the frame and bleeds off all four edges.
```

Then "now cell 05", and so on. Two of them need their brief restating, because a
grid cell cannot show either one properly:

- **20** must be a *seamless, tileable* paper texture at 1024 with no stamp on it
  at all, tiling edge to edge with no visible seam.
- **21** must be square, with the artwork filling the frame and only a small even
  margin — in a contact sheet it tends to come out as a landscape crop.

## The share card is different

Not part of the set and never shown inside the app: the picture that appears when
someone pastes a haunt.place invite or the Vercel URL into a chat. It is the one
image where text belongs, because the text *is* the product.

```
A 1200x630 landscape image. Left 58%: a moody, desaturated photograph of a
coastal town at dusk, real and photographic, quietly graded, very fine film
grain. Right 42%: warm off-white aged paper with visible fibre. Low on the paper,
one small hand-carved multi-colour rubber stamp of a headland and water lines —
misregistered layers, dry ink gaps, grainy pigment, charcoal and grey blue with
one small brick-red roof. Beneath the stamp, four short lines of small typewriter
text, left aligned, slightly uneven as if struck by a machine:

HAUNT
No. 01
private places / passed by hand
2026

No dividing line between the two halves. No logos, no buttons, no UI, no other
text. Spell every word exactly as written.
```

## How the current placeholders were made

The first set came back as ink on paper, so the paper had to come off. A small
script cut the twenty-one cells out of the contact sheet, then keyed each one:
alpha from how much darker than paper a pixel is — which is also what carries the
carving, so dry gaps and grain survive as transparency rather than as colour — and
colour from the pixel's own hue mapped to the nearest state colour in the theme.
Brick red became the friend-of-a-friend tan, deep green the visited sage, grey
blue the unvisited lavender, everything neutral plain ink.

Asking the model for pale ink on black up front (above) skips all of that and
comes back at full resolution. The script stays as the fallback for art that
arrives on paper anyway: [`tools/key-stamps.py`](../tools/key-stamps.py), run
from the repo root against a folder of opaque crops in `public/art`.

## What is in `public/art` right now

Placeholders, cut from the fourth contact sheet with `sips`: each cell tight-cropped
to its paper, the printed number sliced off the top, saved as JPEG. They are about
**240px** and soft on a retina screen — enough to judge the design, not enough to
ship. Full-resolution files drop in under the same names; the only change needed is
the extension in [`src/lib/art.ts`](../src/lib/art.ts), which is the one place a
filename appears.

`sips` on this machine cannot write WebP, which is why the stopgap set is JPEG.

## Output spec

- **Square 1:1** for everything except the share card.
- Generate at 1024; downscale the 640s afterwards. Anything rendering at 160px in
  the app only needs 640 — the whole set at 1024 would be needless weight.
- **WebP, quality ~82**: `cwebp -q 82 in.png -o out.webp`. Expect 60–200KB each.
- The app icon stays **PNG** (`icon.png`), because installers and favicons want
  lossless and some still choke on WebP.
- Crop to the paper before saving. The app expects the paper to bleed to all four
  edges — any background beyond it will show as a hard rectangle.
- Everything goes in `public/art/`, named exactly as in the table.

## Wiring, once they exist

Say the word and I will:

1. Build a `FieldNote` card that lays an image on the dark ground with a hair of
   rotation and a soft shadow, so it reads as paper resting there rather than a
   picture pasted in.
2. Typeset each screen's own copy over or beneath it, from props — never pixels.
3. Replace the gradient orbs in onboarding, sign-in and the recovery screen, then
   fill in the empty states, which today are bare sentences.
4. Preload image 01 so the cold open never shows an empty slot, and lazy-load the
   rest.
5. Wire `paper-tile.webp` as the ground for keepsake cards.

Alt text stays empty (`alt=""`) on all of them — they are decoration, and every
one of these screens already says in words what it means.

## What stays code

Generated art is fixed, so it can never name a place. A **keepsake** is minted
with a real haunt's name, its three vibe tags, its number and its year — which is
precisely a field note, and precisely what a raster cannot be.

For those, the stamp is drawn in the app from the haunt's own data, with the
carving chosen from its seed so a place is always stamped the same way. That is
`src/components/FieldNote.tsx`: SVG-filter texture — noise-displaced edges, a
dry-ink mask, ink grain, per-colour misregistration — over hand-authored motifs.
It will never match a generated raster for richness, but it is a few kilobytes,
it takes live data, and it themes.

The division: **generated art for fixed screens, drawn stamps for anything that
names a real place.**
